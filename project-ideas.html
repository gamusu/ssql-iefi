Project ideas

Tracking and quantitative methods for the 2026–27 IEFI projects

Every project can track something. Many can also build a method into their programming or reflection. Pick the level that fits: light (tracking and a feedback form), medium (a pre/post survey or data-based profile), or deep (a small research design or mapping project).

TipTalk to SSQL early

A 30-minute consultation at the start of your project is much more useful than one the week your reflection is due.

Bearing Witness, From Page to Place

Method: Map trip sites alongside county-level historical data such as population change during the Great Migration, voting access, or school segregation.

Track: Course materials developed; optionally a pre/post survey of enrolled students.

Reflection: What can numbers capture about memory and place, and what can’t they?

Skills: GIS, archival research, mixed methods.

Building Your Identity Through Career Development

Method: A needs-assessment survey of the pilot population on career interests, confidence, and awareness of resources.

Track: Panel attendance, pre/post confidence ratings, Affinity page analytics.

Reflection: Compare what students said they needed with what they used.

Skills: Survey design, user research, web analytics.

Disability Identity, Community, and Support

Method: A structured accessibility audit of spaces, events, or web pages, and a campus-wide survey on accessibility attitudes.

Track: Event attendance and pre/post awareness.

Care: Disability status is often undisclosed; report in aggregate and make the survey itself accessible.

Skills: Accessibility auditing, universal design.

Faculty & Staff Spotlight Series

Method: A brief pre/post belonging measure and a follow-up asking whether students reached out to a featured faculty or staff member.

Track: Attendance by students, faculty, and staff; reach among first-gen, transfer, and underrepresented students.

Reflection: Connect results to the Strayhorn and Tinto research cited in the project.

Skills: Event evaluation, research-to-practice.

Good Trouble & Good Eats

Method: Measure connection before and after, for example with a short validated loneliness scale, or map who connected with whom.

Track: Attendance, repeat participation, and where participants come from on campus.

Reflection: Pair the numbers with the stories shared at the table.

Skills: Program evaluation, social network basics.

IEFI 2.0: Assessing Our Toolkit and Looking at AI Use

Method: Surveys of past fellows and advisors, analysis of program records, and a rubric-based pilot comparing AI tools with current processes.

Track: Time and quality measures during the pilot.

Skills: Program evaluation, organizational and technology assessment.

Redefining Wellness: The Spiritual Awakening Project

Method: A start- and end-of-year awareness survey, such as the share of students who know the Interfaith Center serves nonreligious students.

Track: Attendance at meetups, hands-on projects, and service events; compare with earlier years if records exist.

Care: Keep religious identity questions optional.

Skills: Longitudinal tracking, outreach measurement.

Revitalizing Learning 4 Life

Method: Participation analysis by role and a short barriers survey for non-participants.

Track: Sessions offered and enrollment over time; a map of potential collaborators.

Reflection: Connect to Educational Studies research on cross-generational learning.

Skills: Participation analysis, barrier assessment.

Strong Mind, Strong Body, Strong Spirit

Method: Podcast analytics and a before-and-after survey on attitudes toward seeking mental health support.

Track: Episode reach, drop-off points, listener feedback.

Care: Teams are small; report only in aggregate and work with CAPS on wording.

Skills: Media analytics, audience research.
